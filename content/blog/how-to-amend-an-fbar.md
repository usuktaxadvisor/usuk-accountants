Two questions reach us constantly from Americans in the UK who have just dealt with the FBAR for the first time: *I made a mistake, how do I fix it?* and *I filed, now what?* Both have short, reassuring answers, provided the mistake is an honest one.

## The short answer

**To amend an FBAR you file a complete new FinCEN Form 114, tick the Amend box, and quote the BSA Identifier of the original report. After any FBAR is filed, the only thing that happens is that FinCEN sends an acknowledgement and an identifier; there is no review, assessment or bill. Keep the acknowledgement and your working papers for five years.**

## When an FBAR needs amending

The FBAR reports every non-US financial account in which you had a financial interest or signature authority, with the maximum value of each during the calendar year. The report needs correcting if you:

- left out an account — a UK savings account, an ISA, a joint account with a non-US spouse, a company account you can sign on, or a pension you have decided to report;
- reported the wrong year's balances, or a balance that is materially wrong rather than a rounding difference;
- used the wrong exchange rate — FinCEN asks for the US Treasury year-end rate for the year reported, not the rate on the day of the maximum balance;
- entered the wrong account number, institution or address; or
- filed under the wrong filer name or taxpayer identification number.

A balance that is a few dollars out does not need an amendment. FinCEN asks you to round up to the next whole dollar and accepts that maximum values are estimates based on periodic statements.

## How to file an amended FBAR

1. **Gather the original acknowledgement.** When you filed, the BSA E-Filing System emailed (or posted to its secure messaging area) a fourteen-digit **BSA Identifier** for the report. You need it for the amendment. If you cannot find it, FinCEN's instructions say to enter fourteen zeros in the Prior Report BSA Identifier field.
2. **Complete a new Form 114 in full.** An amended FBAR is not a correction slip. It replaces the original report, so it must list every reportable account for that year, including the ones that were right first time.
3. **Tick the Amend box in Item 1** and enter the Prior Report BSA Identifier.
4. **Submit through the BSA E-Filing System** and keep the new acknowledgement. One amended report per year being corrected; if three years were wrong, file three amendments.

If you used a tax preparer, they can file the amendment for you; the FBAR allows a third-party preparer to sign with your authorisation on FinCEN Form 114a, which you keep rather than send.

## What happens after you file

Less than most people expect. The BSA E-Filing System sends an immediate submission acknowledgement and, normally within a few business days, a second message with the BSA Identifier. That is the whole of the process. FinCEN does not review the report with you, does not issue an assessment, and does not send a confirmation that the report has been "accepted" in any substantive sense. The IRS, which enforces FBAR compliance on FinCEN's behalf, does not contact filers about an ordinary, on-time report.

What you should do afterwards:

- **Keep the acknowledgement and the identifier** with your tax papers. You will need the identifier if you ever amend the report.
- **Keep the records** that support each entry — account names, numbers, institution details and the maximum values — for **five years from the due date** of the report. That is the retention period in the FBAR regulations.
- **Check Form 8938.** It is a separate report, filed with your Form 1040, with higher thresholds. Filing one does not satisfy the other, and the two should agree with each other.
- **Diarise next year.** The FBAR is due on 15 April with an automatic extension to 15 October; no request is needed.

## Amendments that reveal a bigger problem

An amendment is the right fix for an honest error on a report you filed on time. It is not the right tool if the "missing account" also produced income that never appeared on your US return, or if you now realise several years of FBARs were never filed at all. In those cases the choice of route matters: filing late FBARs with a documented reasonable cause, or the IRS Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures where returns also need correcting. Since the IRS withdrew its published Delinquent FBAR Submission Procedures on 1 July 2026, that choice deserves more care than it used to. ## Penalties, briefly

FBAR penalties are civil penalties assessed by the IRS after an examination; nothing is assessed automatically when a report is filed or amended. For penalties assessed from 17 January 2025, the inflation-adjusted maximum is $16,536 per non-willful violation, applied per late report following *Bittner v United States* (2023), and far higher for willful violations. A prompt, voluntary correction of an honest mistake is the opposite of willful conduct, which is why it is worth making.
