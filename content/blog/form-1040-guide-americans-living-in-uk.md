If you are a US citizen or green card holder living in the UK, you almost certainly still have a US tax return to file each year. This guide walks through the practical side: whether you need to file, when, what to gather, and how the UK and US returns fit together. It is written for people doing this for the first time, and it sticks to what the IRS, FinCEN and HMRC publish.

## The short answer

**Americans in the UK file Form 1040 every year on their worldwide income, in US dollars. If you were living abroad on 15 April you get until 15 June to file without asking, and until 15 October if you file Form 4868. UK tax you have paid is normally relieved through the Foreign Tax Credit or the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion, and your UK accounts are reported separately on the FBAR.**

## Step 1: Check whether you have to file

The IRS says US citizens and resident aliens living abroad follow the same filing rules as people in the US, and that worldwide income is taxable wherever you live. It makes no difference that your salary is paid in pounds or that HMRC has already taxed it.

You must file once your gross income reaches the threshold for your filing status. For the 2025 tax year the IRS figures are:

| Filing status | Under 65 | 65 or older |
|---|---|---|
| Single | $15,750 | $17,550 |
| Married filing jointly (both spouses the same age band) | $31,500 | $34,700 |
| Married filing separately | $5 | $5 |
| Head of household | $23,625 | $25,625 |

Two points catch people out. If you are married to someone who is not a US person and you file separately, the threshold is $5, so in practice you file. And if you are self-employed, you must file once your net earnings are more than $400, whatever your total income.

You also need a Social Security number, or an ITIN if you are not eligible for one, to file.

## Step 2: Know your dates

The US tax year is the calendar year. The return is due on 15 April of the following year, but people living abroad get more time to file.

| | 2025 return | 2026 return |
|---|---|---|
| Regular due date, and the date interest starts | 15 April 2026 | 15 April 2027 |
| Automatic extension if you live abroad | 15 June 2026 | 15 June 2027 |
| Extension with Form 4868 | 15 October 2026 | 15 October 2027 |

The June extension needs no form. You attach a short statement to the return saying that you were living outside the US on the due date. The IRS can also allow a further two months, to 15 December, if you write explaining why you need it before 15 October.

The extensions give you more time to file, not more time to pay. If any US tax is due, interest runs from 15 April.

The UK side runs on a different calendar. The UK tax year is 6 April to 5 April, and the online Self Assessment return and payment for 2025/26 are due by 31 January 2027. One US return therefore draws on parts of two UK tax years, which is the main reason the figures on the two returns never match line for line.

## Step 3: Gather what the return needs

Most of what a US return needs is already in your UK paperwork. For a typical year, have these to hand:

- Your P60, and your payslips for January to December, because the US year does not follow the UK tax year
- Interest and dividend statements from every bank, building society and investment account
- Rental income and expenses, if you let property
- Your accounts or records, if you are self-employed
- Pension statements, including any payments you received
- The highest balance during the year for each non-US account
- Your UK tax calculation or Self Assessment return for the years that overlap
- Last year's US return, if you filed one

If you sold a home, shares or funds, you also need the purchase and sale dates and amounts, because gains are worked out in dollars at the rates on those dates.

## Step 4: Convert to US dollars

Everything on a US return is in dollars. The IRS does not set an official rate and generally accepts any posted exchange rate that you use consistently.

- For income that arrives evenly through the year, such as salary, you may use the yearly average rate for that tax year. The IRS publishes one.
- For a single transaction, such as selling a property, use the rate for that day.

Pick one approach and keep to it from year to year.

## Step 5: Deal with tax already paid in the UK

There are two main ways the US return takes account of UK tax, and you cannot use both on the same income.

**The Foreign Tax Credit (Form 1116)** gives a credit against US tax for income tax you paid to the UK on the same income. Credit you cannot use is carried back one year and then forward for up to ten. Our [Foreign Tax Credit page](/services/us-expat-tax/foreign-tax-credit) explains how we apply it.

**The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (Form 2555)** takes earned income out of US tax up to a yearly limit: $130,000 for 2025 and $132,900 for 2026. It covers earnings from work only, and you have to qualify under one of the IRS residence tests. See our [Foreign Earned Income Exclusion page](/services/us-expat-tax/foreign-earned-income-exclusion).

Which suits you depends on what kind of income you have and how much UK tax you pay on it. The [exclusion or credit comparison](/resources/blog/feie-or-foreign-tax-credit-uk) goes through the choice.

## Step 6: Add the reports that go with the return

For many Americans in the UK the reports matter as much as the return itself.

**FBAR (FinCEN Form 114).** Required if the combined value of your non-US financial accounts was more than $10,000 at any time in the calendar year. It is filed online with FinCEN, not with your tax return. It is due on 15 April, with an automatic extension to 15 October. See [FBAR filing](/services/us-expat-tax/fbar-filing).

**Form 8938.** Filed with the return when your foreign financial assets pass a higher threshold. If you live abroad that is more than $200,000 on the last day of the year or $300,000 at any time for a single filer, and $400,000 or $600,000 for a married couple filing jointly. See [FATCA and Form 8938](/services/us-expat-tax/fatca-compliance).

**Other forms.** Owning shares in a UK company can bring [Form 5471](/services/us-expat-tax/form-5471) into play, and UK funds held in an ISA or a general account have their own reporting. Our [ISA and UK investments guide](/resources/blog/why-is-my-isa-a-problem-for-us-taxes) explains why.

## Step 7: File

Most returns can be filed electronically. The IRS notes that only a limited number of software providers can handle a foreign address, so check before you start. A paper return with no payment enclosed goes to the Department of the Treasury, Internal Revenue Service, Austin, TX 73301-0215, USA.

Whichever way you file, keep a copy of the return, the FBAR confirmation and your workings. You will want them next year.

## Which schedules you are likely to meet

- **Schedule B** for interest and dividends. It also asks whether you hold accounts outside the US.
- **Schedule C** for self-employment income and expenses.
- **Schedule D** for capital gains and losses.
- **Schedule E** for rental income.
- **Schedule SE** for self-employment tax. If you are self-employed in the UK, read our [guide for self-employed Americans](/resources/blog/self-employed-in-uk-us-tax-obligations) before assuming it applies.

## If you also need a UK return

Not everyone in the UK files Self Assessment, but many Americans do: the self-employed, landlords, and people with income that PAYE does not reach.
